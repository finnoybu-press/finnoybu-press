For the first time, you can describe a physical object in a sentence and have a machine hand you something three-dimensional a few seconds later. Type "a low-poly fox sitting upright" and a generative model hands you a textured fox. Describe "a flange with six bolt holes on a 100-millimeter circle" to an AI-CAD tool and it hands you something an engineer could open and edit. People who have never touched a 3D program are making 3D things this afternoon.

What almost nobody tells you is the honest part: a model that *looks* 3D is not the same as a model you can *print*, *edit*, or *manufacture*. The pretty preview may be a hollow shell no printer can use, a tangled mesh no artist can edit, or a "CAD" file that is really a mesh in disguise. *AI for 3D Modeling* teaches the one habit that fixes all of that: asking which rung of the file-format ladder a model is really on.

## Inside, you'll learn how to

- Use the file-format ladder (GLB, STL and 3MF, STEP) to know at a glance what a tool's output is good for
- Choose among the generative tools, from Meshy and Tripo to Hunyuan3D, Rodin, TRELLIS, Hitem3D, and MakerWorld's MakerLab, by what each is genuinely best at
- Turn a generated mesh into a successful print: cleanup, watertightness, scale, and the slicer
- Use AI-native CAD (Zoo and its Zookeeper agent, Adam, Leo AI, and the copilots inside Fusion, SOLIDWORKS, and Onshape) and tell real editable geometry from a mesh in a trench coat
- Know where AI 3D breaks, who owns a generated model, and when you still need a human modeler or engineer

**Who it's for:** makers and 3D-printing hobbyists, designers and indie game developers, founders with an idea for a physical thing, and engineers who want a clear-eyed read on what AI-CAD can really do today. No CAD license or 3D experience required.
